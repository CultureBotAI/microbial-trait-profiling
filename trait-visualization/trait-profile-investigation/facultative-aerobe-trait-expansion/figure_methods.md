# Figure Methods

These method notes describe how each PNG in this investigation folder was generated by `scripts/investigate_facultative_aerobe_expansion.py`.

## Shared Inputs

Study-profile z-scores were read from `trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_clustered_heatmap_matrix.tsv` and joined to study metadata from `trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_study_metadata.tsv`. The facultative-aerobe guild score was the row-wise mean of the selected guild traits: facultatively aerobic, aerobic, gram negative, motile, microaerophilic, and nitrate_reduction.
Direction labels (`increased` and `decreased`) were taken from BugSigDB's curated `Abundance in Group 1` signature field. This script did not recalculate differential abundance; it grouped and filtered BugSigDB-reported study/experiment/direction profiles by condition and body-site metadata.

The focal taxa were defined a priori in the script's `TARGET_TAXA` map using NCBI Taxonomy identifiers, which avoids ambiguity from synonym or strain-name differences. The selected taxa were Parvimonas micra (NCBITaxon:33033), Klebsiella pneumoniae (NCBITaxon:573), Streptococcus anginosus (NCBITaxon:1328), Escherichia coli (NCBITaxon:562), Fusobacterium nucleatum (NCBITaxon:851), Enterocloster bolteae (NCBITaxon:208479), Eggerthella lenta (NCBITaxon:84112), Prevotella intermedia (NCBITaxon:28131), Porphyromonas gingivalis (NCBITaxon:837), and Veillonella dispar (NCBITaxon:39778). They were chosen as focal examples for the facultative-aerobe increased-profile signal because they include taxa with oxygen-tolerance, gram-negative, motility, microaerophilic, or nitrate-reduction annotations in the derived taxa-trait matrix, and because they provide gut, oral, and airway contexts where facultative-aerobe or aerotolerant increased-profile patterns may be relevant. The script writes `candidate_taxa_trait_presence.tsv` from `data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv` as the audit trail for those trait annotations; some focal taxa carry broad multi-trait support, while others are retained as single-trait focal taxa for condition/body-site follow-up.

## `candidate_taxa_increased_profile_tendency.png`

This figure uses the fixed focal taxa listed above, then intersects their NCBI Taxonomy IDs with `trait-visualization/taxa_guild_discovery_taxa_metadata.tsv`. That metadata table supplies the total BugSigDB profile count and the number of profiles where each taxon was reported as increased or decreased. For each target taxon, increased-profile tendency was calculated as `(increased profile count - decreased profile count) / total BugSigDB profile count`. The horizontal bars show this signed value for each target taxon. No condition or body-site filter is applied to this figure; it summarizes the overall BugSigDB direction tendency of the preselected focal taxa.

## `condition_increased_guild_signal.png`

Study profiles from all body sites were grouped by BugSigDB condition label. Groups with fewer than five profiles were excluded. Broad non-condition or metadata labels (`Body fat distribution`, `Exposure`, `Health trait`, `Microbiome`, `Population`, `Physical activity`, and labels containing `exposure`, `measurement`, or `population`) were removed from the displayed condition summary. Those labels usually describe exposure, cohort, or measurement metadata rather than a disease or condition context. The figure keeps the top 12 eligible conditions with at least three increased profiles and at least two studies, using fallback filtering if needed. Bars are sorted high to low by mean facultative-aerobe guild z-score among increased profiles.

## `guild_trait_direction_shift.png`

For each selected guild trait, the script calculated the mean z-score in all increased study profiles and the mean z-score in all decreased study profiles. Bars show `increased mean z - decreased mean z`; positive values indicate traits that are higher in increased profiles.

## `anatomical_sites_diagram.png`

Study profiles were grouped by `body_site_group`, requiring at least 10 profiles per group. The display excludes the generic `Other` body-site group. Bubble and bar length encode the number of study profiles in the body-site group. Color encodes the mean facultative-aerobe guild z-score among increased profiles at that body site. Body-site labels were placed on a simplified, neutral front-view body outline using fixed anatomical coordinates for feces/stool, colon, caecum, oral, airway, urogenital, and unknown labels. Unknown body site means the BugSigDB profile did not have a specific body-site label; these profiles are placed off-body and should not be interpreted as a physical anatomical location.

Oral-site increased-profile signals are biologically plausible because the oral cavity is oxygen-exposed and includes aerotolerant, facultatively aerobic, or microaerophilic taxa such as Streptococcus, Fusobacterium, Prevotella, Porphyromonas, and Veillonella. Those oral-site signals should be interpreted as body-site-specific microbial ecology, not as a gut facultative-aerobe increased-profile signal.

## `target_taxa_condition_breakdown_bar.png`

Target-taxon occurrences were loaded from `data/derived/taxa.tsv`, limited to the fixed focal NCBI Taxonomy IDs, deduplicated by taxon and study profile, and joined to the scored study profiles. This produces one row per focal taxon/profile occurrence with condition, body site, direction, and guild-score metadata. The bar chart counts increased focal-taxon/profile occurrences by condition after removing the broad non-condition and metadata labels described above. A condition with multiple focal taxa can therefore contribute multiple occurrences; the chart is an occurrence breakdown, not a unique-study breakdown. It displays the top 20 explicit condition labels as horizontal bars, with bar labels showing occurrence counts.
