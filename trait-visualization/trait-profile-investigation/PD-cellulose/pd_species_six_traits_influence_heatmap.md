# PD Species-by-Six-Trait Influence Heatmap

![Heatmap](pd_species_six_traits_influence_heatmap.png)

Includes 8 reported species - the lowest reliable rank in the PD carrier data - and all six lollipop traits. Each trait cell is now specific to the species-trait combination: the analysis removes the species' complete non-MediaDive annotation vector from profiles where it was reported, recomputes the six log-fraction scores using the unchanged human-corpus reference, forms paired increased-minus-decreased experiment contrasts, averages experiments within study, and weights studies equally. Species whose six cells are all low support are omitted. Outlined cells include direct removal of the displayed trait; unoutlined cells show the denominator-mediated effect when the species has no direct annotation for that trait. The taxon row label also includes the BugSigDB PD increased/decreased profile count, such as `(0/13)`.

Rows are sorted from the most increased-skewed to the most decreased-skewed BugSigDB PD recurrence using the log2 increased/decreased profile-count ratio; ties use the signed count difference, total support, and taxon name.

`inc` and `dec` require at least 3 supporting studies and a 95% Student t interval entirely above or below zero. The heatmap prints signed numeric fixed-reference z influence values in supported cells; `mixed` is retained in the TSV when the interval crosses zero. Hatched `low support` cells do not receive a forced direction. The first right-hand annotation retains the raw increased/decreased profile occurrence counts in PD feces. The second annotation shows the DOI-backed literature direction and explicitly labels whether BugSigDB recurrence agrees, differs, or is balanced. `DIFFERS` marks *Mediterraneibacter torques* (BugSigDB 3/1 increased versus literature decreased) and *Escherichia coli* (BugSigDB 1/2 decreased versus literature increased); *Ruminococcus bromii* is marked balanced. PD-versus-other-feces specificity is retained in the companion audit TSV but is no longer plotted.

The generated reference table joins literature direction, DOI citations, BugSigDB PD occurrence direction, KG-Microbe six-trait annotations, strongest influence value, and an interpretation of agreement, disagreement, or balanced evidence.

## Methods: Literature Review and Direction Comparison

The literature direction table is a targeted narrative review updated 2026-09-22; it is not a systematic review or meta-analysis. PubMed, PubMed Central, and publisher records were searched using `Parkinson disease`, `gut microbiome`, each taxon name or synonym, and direction terms (`increased`, `decreased`, `enriched`, or `depleted`). Existing DOI-backed PD microbiome references were checked backward for additional direction evidence.

Eligible evidence comprised human PD-versus-control fecal or intestinal microbiome studies and cross-cohort human meta-analyses that reported an explicit taxon direction. Animal-only, intervention-only, non-gut, and severity-only findings were excluded from increased/decreased calls. Association-only evidence is retained as `associated` and is not treated as directional.

Literature direction is assigned at the most specific supported taxonomic level. An exact species result is preferred; otherwise a genus or family result is used as a flagged parent-level proxy. `variable` is used when eligible reports conflict at the matched level. The reference table records the matched term, reference rank, match type, evidence statement, and DOI list so broader-rank comparisons remain visible.

BugSigDB direction is a recurrence summary, not a pooled abundance effect: `increased` when increased-profile count is greater than decreased-profile count, `decreased` for the reverse, and `balanced` for a tie. `agrees` and `differs` are assigned only against directional literature calls; balanced, variable, associated, and unlisted cases remain separate. These labels do not test statistical agreement and do not resolve cohort, medication, geography, sequencing, or taxonomic-version heterogeneity.

This is a reported-species influence diagnostic, not a causal attribution or strain-level measurement. Removing a reported species changes both trait numerators and the nonlinear full denominator. Parent/child propagation and unresolved source assertions remain the limitations documented in #190; contrast orientation and trait semantics remain bounded by #188 and #189.
