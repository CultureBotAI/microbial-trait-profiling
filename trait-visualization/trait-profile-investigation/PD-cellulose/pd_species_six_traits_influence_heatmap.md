# PD Species-by-Six-Trait Influence Heatmap

![Heatmap](pd_species_six_traits_influence_heatmap.png)

Includes 5 reported species - the lowest reliable rank in the PD carrier data - and all six lollipop traits. Each trait cell is now specific to the species-trait combination: the analysis removes the species' complete non-MediaDive annotation vector from profiles where it was reported, recomputes the six log-fraction scores using the unchanged human-corpus reference, forms paired increased-minus-decreased experiment contrasts, averages experiments within study, and weights studies equally. Species whose six cells are all low support or whose six mean influences remain between -0.01 and +0.01 are omitted. *Mediterraneibacter torques* is explicitly excluded. For retained rows, only cell values greater than +0.01 or less than -0.01 are drawn; the exact values remain in the cell TSV with a `displayed_in_heatmap` flag. Outlined cells include direct removal of the displayed trait; unoutlined cells show the denominator-mediated effect when the species has no direct annotation for that trait. Thus, a visible outlined cell means the organism has a direct trait annotation and its removal changes the paired PD contrast beyond the display threshold. This is contribution/influence evidence, not a causal claim. The row label also includes the BugSigDB PD increased/decreased profile count, such as `(0/13)`.

The x-axis value labeled `baseline Δz` is the full-data, study-weighted paired PD increased-minus-decreased trait contrast before removing any row taxon. The heatmap cell is that baseline Δz minus the contrast recomputed after removing the taxon. The cell values therefore explain sensitivity of the column-level contrast, but they do not sum to it because profile fractions are nonlinear and taxon annotations overlap.

Each column also carries the trait's observed direction in the PD six-trait lollipop, the sign of `increased_minus_decreased_mean_z` in `pd_six_trait_direction_summary.tsv`: an up arrow where the trait's mean z is higher in increased profiles and a down arrow where it is higher in decreased profiles, with the difference labeled `lollipop`. The trait and cell tables carry it as `trait_direction`. The lollipop value is an unpaired difference of profile means, while baseline Δz pairs profiles within experiments and weights studies equally, so the two can differ in size and, for traits near zero, in sign; an asterisk after the baseline Δz marks such a column, and the trait table's `baseline_opposes_lollipop` flags it. A positive cell pushes the paired contrast toward increased profiles and a negative cell toward decreased profiles, whatever the column's arrow.

Rows are sorted from the most increased-skewed to the most decreased-skewed BugSigDB PD recurrence using the log2 increased/decreased profile-count ratio; ties use the signed count difference, total support, and taxon name.

`inc` and `dec` require at least 3 supporting studies and a 95% Student t interval entirely above or below zero. The heatmap prints signed numeric fixed-reference z influence values in supported cells; `mixed` is retained in the TSV when the interval crosses zero. Hatched `low support` cells do not receive a forced direction. The first right-hand annotation retains the raw increased/decreased profile occurrence counts in PD feces. The second annotation shows the DOI-backed literature direction using red for increased and blue for decreased. Directional literature and BugSigDB recurrence are compared with one-position Hamming distance: 0 means similar and gets a green border; 1 means different and gets a red border. Balanced and non-directional comparisons have no defined Hamming distance. PD-versus-other-feces specificity is retained in the companion audit TSV but is no longer plotted.

The generated reference table joins literature direction, DOI citations, BugSigDB PD occurrence direction, KG-Microbe six-trait annotations, strongest influence value, and an interpretation of agreement, disagreement, or balanced evidence.

## Methods: Literature Review and Direction Comparison

The literature direction table is a targeted narrative review updated 2026-09-22; it is not a systematic review or meta-analysis. PubMed, PubMed Central, and publisher records were searched using `Parkinson disease`, `gut microbiome`, each taxon name or synonym, and direction terms (`increased`, `decreased`, `enriched`, or `depleted`). Existing DOI-backed PD microbiome references were checked backward for additional direction evidence.

Eligible evidence comprised human PD-versus-control fecal or intestinal microbiome studies and cross-cohort human meta-analyses that reported an explicit taxon direction. Animal-only, intervention-only, non-gut, and severity-only findings were excluded from increased/decreased calls. Association-only evidence is retained as `associated` and is not treated as directional.

Literature direction is assigned at the most specific supported taxonomic level. An exact species result is preferred; otherwise a genus or family result is used as a flagged parent-level proxy. `variable` is used when eligible reports conflict at the matched level. The reference table records the matched term, reference rank, match type, evidence statement, and DOI list so broader-rank comparisons remain visible.

BugSigDB direction is a recurrence summary, not a pooled abundance effect: `increased` when increased-profile count is greater than decreased-profile count, `decreased` for the reverse, and `balanced` for a tie. `agrees` and `differs` are assigned only against directional literature calls. For those calls, literature and BugSigDB direction are each encoded as one binary categorical position and compared with Hamming distance: 0 means the directions are similar and 1 means they differ. Balanced, variable, associated, and unlisted cases have no defined Hamming distance and remain separate. This is a descriptive direction check, not a statistical test of agreement, and it does not resolve cohort, medication, geography, sequencing, or taxonomic-version heterogeneity.

This is a reported-species influence diagnostic, not a causal attribution or strain-level measurement. Removing a reported species changes both trait numerators and the nonlinear full denominator. Parent/child propagation and unresolved source assertions remain the limitations documented in #190; contrast orientation and trait semantics remain bounded by #188 and #189.
