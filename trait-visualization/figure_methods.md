# Figure Methods

This is the canonical consolidated methods file for figures in `trait-visualization/` and downstream outputs in `trait-visualization/trait-profile-investigation/`. It accumulates the methods text that previously lived in separate `*figure_methods.md` files, while preserving the organization by analysis family, current presentation figures, supplemental outputs, and archived source-method appendices.

Terminology convention: traits are described as enriched or depleted. BugSigDB profile directions are described as increased and decreased profiles. Backward-compatible filenames, folder names, TSV columns, and raw BugSigDB source labels may still contain `guild`, `depletion`, `inc`, or `dec` when those strings are part of an existing artifact or source identifier.

The figures were generated from BugSigDB-derived taxa and study-profile tables plus KG-Microbe trait annotations. Direction labels come from BugSigDB curation, specifically the `Abundance in Group 1` signature direction; the visualization scripts group, filter, aggregate, and cluster those curated directions rather than recalculating differential abundance.

## Clustered Heatmaps

### `taxa_guild_discovery_clustered_heatmap.png`

This figure is a taxa-by-trait clustered heatmap for discovering groups of
BugSigDB-relevant taxa with similar biological, ecological, or phenotypic trait
annotations. Rows are selected BugSigDB leaf-rank taxa and columns are KG-Microbe
trait columns. The current rendered matrix contains 80 species-level taxa and 45
traits.

The figure was generated by `scripts/create_taxa_guild_heatmap.py`. The script reads
BugSigDB taxon occurrences from `data/raw/full_dump.csv` when available, using the raw
export to parse taxon names, taxonomic ranks, study IDs, experiment IDs, signature IDs,
and BugSigDB abundance direction labels. If the raw export is unavailable, it falls
back to `data/derived/taxa.tsv`. Taxon-trait annotations are read from
`data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv`, and trait labels/categories are read from
`data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix_columns.tsv`. The script also reads
`data/derived/study_trait_profiles_wide.tsv` to require that selected traits have
support in study-profile data, not only in the taxa-trait matrix.

The script requests `strain` rows by default and falls back to `species` when strain
rows are absent; the current BugSigDB raw export contains no MetaPhlAn `t__` strain
leaves, so this heatmap uses species-level taxa. Full-dump rows are restricted to
`Homo sapiens` experiments before taxon support is summarized. Taxa were filtered to
those with at least 4 BugSigDB study/experiment/direction profiles and at least 5
annotated traits, then ranked by BugSigDB profile count, study count, and trait count.
At most 80 taxa are plotted. Traits were filtered to those present in at least 4
selected taxa and at least 4 study profiles. MediaDive growth-medium traits are excluded unless
`--include-media-traits` is supplied. Trait ranking uses
`selected-taxa support * log1p(study-profile support)`, and the selected matrix is
binary: `1` means the trait is annotated for the taxon and `0` means absent or not
annotated.

Rows and columns were clustered with seaborn `clustermap` using average linkage and
Jaccard distance on the binary matrix. Teal cells indicate trait presence and white
cells indicate absence/no annotation. Row colors summarize each taxon's overall
BugSigDB direction tendency across profiles: `increased` if increased profiles are at
least 2 and at least 1.25 times decreased profiles, `decreased` by the analogous rule,
and `mixed` otherwise. Column colors show trait category.

- `taxa_guild_discovery_trait_metadata.tsv`

### `study_trait_profile_clustered_heatmap.png`

This figure clusters BugSigDB study/experiment/direction profiles by aggregate trait
composition. Each source row represents one study, one experiment, and one curated
profile direction (`increased` or `decreased`; stored in profile IDs as
`inc` or `dec`), so increased and decreased profiles are not merged before
clustering. The current full matrix contains 5,829 human-host profiles from 1,080
studies and 60 selected traits. The PNG rendering averages adjacent clustered profiles into 320
direction-specific display bins so that the full dataset remains legible.

The figure was generated by `scripts/create_study_trait_profile_heatmap.py`. The
script reads one wide profile table from
`data/derived/study_trait_profiles_wide.tsv`, readable trait metadata from
`data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix_columns.tsv`, study titles and compact study metadata
from `data/derived/studies.tsv`, and condition/body-site/location/host-species labels
from `data/raw/full_dump.csv`. Profile IDs are built as
`study_id|experiment_id|inc_dec`. The `inc_dec` labels map to BugSigDB-reported
`increased` and `decreased` directions. Experiment-level host-species labels from
`full_dump.csv` are used to restrict the plotted profile corpus to `Homo sapiens`.

By default, all profiles are kept (`--max-profiles 0`) with no minimum trait-count or
total-signal filter. Traits are selected to emphasize variable, interpretable columns:
they must be present in at least 6 profiles, present in no more than 95% of selected
profiles, and have nonzero composition variance. MediaDive growth-medium traits are
excluded unless `--include-media-traits` is supplied. Traits are ranked by
`composition variance * log1p(total signal)`, and at most 60 traits are plotted.

For each profile row, raw trait counts are converted to fractions of that profile's
total trait signal. Fractions are scaled per 1,000 profile counts and transformed with
`log1p`. Each selected trait column is then standardized to a z-score across plotted
profiles. The matrix TSV stores these full, unbinned z-scores. In the PNG, the color
scale is centered at zero and capped at +/-1: red indicates a higher-than-average
trait fraction for that row, blue indicates lower-than-average, and near-white values
are close to the column mean. The colors are relative trait-profile signals and do not
mean good/bad or increased/decreased abundance.

Rows are ordered by direction section. Increased profiles are clustered as one block,
decreased profiles are clustered as a second block, and the blocks are kept separate
with increased profiles shown first. Within each direction block, row ordering uses
average-linkage hierarchical clustering with Euclidean distance. The PNG display bins
are produced by splitting each direction-specific clustered order into adjacent bins
and averaging profile z-scores within each bin. Columns are clustered with average
linkage and correlation distance. Row annotations show direction, grouped body site,
and grouped condition. Column annotations show trait category.

Companion files:

- `study_trait_profile_clustered_heatmap_matrix.tsv`
- `study_trait_profile_study_metadata.tsv`
- `study_trait_profile_trait_metadata.tsv`

### `study_trait_profile_paired_enrichment_heatmap.png`

This paired companion heatmap applies the matched enrichment statistic to the same
selected study-profile trait z-scores. The script keeps study/experiment pairs that
have both an increased and a decreased BugSigDB profile. For each matched
study/experiment pair and each selected trait, the plotted statistic is `increased
profile z-score - decreased profile z-score`. Positive values therefore indicate
traits enriched in the increased profile relative to the decreased profile from the
same study and experiment; negative values indicate the reverse. This paired contrast
removes between-study baseline differences before visual clustering.

The current paired matrix contains 2,335 matched study/experiment pairs from 959
studies and the same 60 selected traits. The full paired matrix is written without
display binning. The PNG rendering orders matched-pair rows by average-linkage
hierarchical clustering with Euclidean distance on the paired enrichment vectors,
then averages adjacent clustered pairs into 320 display bins by default. Columns are
clustered with average linkage and correlation distance, so traits are grouped by
similarity of their enrichment/depletion pattern across matched pairs. The color scale
is centered at zero and capped at +/-1 for display.

Companion files:

- `study_trait_profile_paired_enrichment_matrix.tsv`
- `study_trait_profile_paired_enrichment_metadata.tsv`
- `study_trait_profile_paired_required_trait_check.tsv`

The paired heatmap generator also requires three requested profile traits to be
present in the selected trait set: `mesophilic` (`METPO:1000615`), `gram positive`
(`METPO:1000698`), and `non motile` (`METPO:1000703`). If any of these traits no
longer passes the heatmap selection filters, generation fails instead of silently
dropping the requested check. The companion required-trait TSV records each
trait's profile support, selection score, inclusion status, and matched-pair
increased-minus-decreased summary.

### `condition_trait_module_clustered_heatmap.png`

This figure clusters condition/body-site modules by aggregate microbial trait
composition. It is intended to highlight condition-level trait signatures after
aggregating study profiles within clinically meaningful metadata groups. The current
matrix contains 80 condition/body-site modules and 60 selected traits; the selected
modules summarize 536 studies and 2,697 study/experiment/direction profiles from
the human-host corpus.

The companion diagram `condition_trait_module_guild_definition_table.png` is a
presentation-friendly replacement for the dense heatmap when the goal is to explain
guild logic rather than inspect every module-by-trait value. It uses `X OR Y OR Z`
for taxon support and supporting-taxa evidence, while profile and condition-module
signals remain continuous summaries: `mean z(X, Y, Z)` over the selected trait
columns. `X AND Y AND Z` should only be used for a deliberately strict definition
where all listed traits are required; it is not the default for broad KG-Microbe
phenotype guilds.

The figure was generated by `scripts/create_condition_trait_module_heatmap.py`. The
script reads `data/derived/study_trait_profiles_wide.tsv`,
`data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix_columns.tsv`, `data/derived/studies.tsv`, and
`data/raw/full_dump.csv`. Increased and decreased experiment profiles are first summed
to study-level trait profiles. Studies are then grouped by `condition` and `body_site`
by default. Unknown grouping labels are excluded unless `--include-unknown-groups` is
supplied.

Groups are eligible if they contain at least 2 studies, at least 4 source profiles,
at least 8 traits, and total trait signal of at least 40. Eligible groups are ranked
by study count, profile count, total trait signal, and trait count, and at most 80
groups are plotted. Traits are selected from group-level composition values. A trait
must be present in at least 4 modules, present in no more than 95% of modules, and
have nonzero composition variance. MediaDive growth-medium traits are excluded unless
`--include-media-traits` is supplied. Traits are ranked by
`composition variance * log1p(total signal)`, and at most 60 are plotted.

For each module, raw trait counts are converted to fractions of that module's total
trait signal. Fractions are scaled per 1,000 counts, transformed with `log1p`, and
standardized within each trait column. Values are clipped to +/-2.5 before plotting.
The heatmap uses a red/blue diverging color scale centered at zero: red means the
module has a higher-than-average value for that trait relative to other plotted
modules, blue means lower-than-average, and pale values are near the column mean.

Rows and columns are clustered with seaborn `clustermap` using average linkage and
correlation distance. Row labels are formatted as `condition | body site | study count`.
Row annotations show grouped body site and module-size category (`2`, `3-5`, `6-10`,
or `11+` studies). Column annotations show trait category.

### `bsdb-taxa-only/clustered_heatmap_genus_top100.png`

This figure is an earlier taxa-only presence/absence heatmap. It does not use
KG-Microbe trait annotations. Rows are BugSigDB study/experiment/direction profiles
and columns are prevalent BugSigDB genus labels. The current matrix contains 300
profiles and 100 genera; 170 rendered profiles are increased and 130 are decreased.

The figure was generated by `scripts/create_clustered_heatmap.py` from
`data/derived/taxa.tsv`. The script filters taxa to genus rank, maps BugSigDB
`direction` values to compact `inc`/`dec` labels, and builds profile IDs as
`study_id|experiment_id|inc_dec`. Duplicate profile/genus pairs are collapsed so that
one genus contributes once per profile. The top 100 genera are selected by the number
of unique profiles containing the genus.

The matrix is binary: `1` means the genus appears in that profile and `0` means it
does not. If more than 300 profiles remain after genus filtering, the rendered heatmap
keeps the 300 profiles with the highest genus richness for readability. Rows and
columns are clustered with average linkage and Jaccard distance. Teal cells indicate
genus presence and white cells indicate absence. Row colors show BugSigDB direction:
red for increased and blue for decreased.

### `bsdb-taxa-only/condition_taxon_clustered_heatmap_top40x100.png`

This figure is an earlier condition-by-genus prevalence heatmap. It shows which
prevalent genera recur across studies assigned to common BugSigDB condition labels.
Rows are conditions and columns are genera. The current matrix contains 40 conditions
and 100 genera; displayed conditions have 13 to 81 studies.

The figure was generated by `scripts/create_condition_taxon_heatmap.py`. The script
extracts unique study-condition pairs from `data/raw/full_dump.csv` and reads genus
occurrences from `data/derived/taxa.tsv`. It keeps the 40 most common condition
labels by unique study count. Within those conditions, it keeps the 100 genera with
the highest unique-study support.

For each condition/genus pair, the cell value is the number of studies in that
condition containing the genus divided by the total number of studies assigned to
that condition. Rows and columns are clustered with average linkage and Euclidean
distance. The heatmap uses a `YlGnBu` sequential color scale, where darker cells mean
a larger fraction of studies in that condition contain the genus. Row labels include
the condition name and the number of studies assigned to that condition.

### Parkinson's Disease Follow-Up Analysis

The Parkinson's disease follow-up figures are in
`trait-profile-investigation/parkinsons-disease/` and were generated by
`scripts/analyze_parkinsons_disease_trait_profiles.py`. They apply the two
recommended Parkinson's-specific follow-up analyses: feces-only trait-score
contrasts and target-taxa direction consistency.

The analysis filters the study-profile metadata to exact condition
`Parkinson's disease` and body-site group `Feces`. The current filtered dataset
contains 352 BugSigDB study/experiment/direction profiles: 182 increased
profiles and 170 decreased profiles from 70 studies. BugSigDB profile directions
are the curated increased/decreased directions already present in
`study_trait_profile_study_metadata.tsv`; the follow-up analysis does not
recalculate differential abundance.

Aerobe and anaerobe profile scores are computed from the same v2 trait selections
used by the corresponding trait-profile investigation folders. The script reads
the selected trait columns from
`aerobe-enrichment-v2/guild_trait_selection.tsv` and
`anaerobe-depletion-v2/guild_trait_selection.tsv`, maps heatmap display labels to
trait object names with `study_trait_profile_trait_metadata.tsv`, and computes
each profile's row-wise mean z-score across the selected traits. Positive-trait
counts are the number of selected trait z-scores greater than zero in that
profile.

`parkinsons_feces_profile_trait_score_contrast.png` compares profile-level v2
scores between decreased and increased Parkinson's feces profiles. Each dot is
one BugSigDB study/experiment/direction profile, boxes show score distributions,
black diamonds show means, and the dashed horizontal line marks zero. The aerobe
panel is interpreted as increased profiles having higher aerobe enrichment scores
when orange values exceed blue values. The anaerobe panel is interpreted in the
opposite direction: decreased profiles having higher anaerobe depletion scores
when blue values exceed orange values.

`parkinsons_feces_paired_trait_contrast.png` keeps only matched study/experiment
pairs that have both increased and decreased Parkinson's feces profiles. The
aerobe contrast is `increased score - decreased score`; the anaerobe contrast is
`decreased score - increased score`; the combined reciprocal signal is the sum of
those two contrasts. Positive values indicate the expected reciprocal pattern:
aerobe signal higher in increased profiles and anaerobe signal higher in
decreased profiles. Dots are matched experiments and black diamonds are mean
contrasts.

Profile-level comparisons in `parkinsons_feces_trait_score_tests.tsv` use
two-sided Mann-Whitney U tests between the focus direction and comparator
direction. Paired contrasts use two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests against
zero. These tests summarize recurrence patterns in curated signatures; they are
not causal models and do not adjust for study design, medication, geography, or
sequencing platform.

`parkinsons_target_taxa_direction_bar.png` summarizes Parkinson's feces rows from
the aerobe and anaerobe v2 `candidate_taxa_condition_direction.tsv` files.
Orange bars to the right count increased profiles and blue bars to the left
count decreased profiles. The left panel shows aerobe-enrichment target taxa and
the right panel shows anaerobe-depletion target taxa. Long one-sided bars
indicate stronger direction consistency for a candidate taxon.

`parkinsons_anaerobe_depletion_taxa_support.png` is a focused view of the
anaerobe-depletion target taxa. Horizontal bars compare decreased and increased
profile support, while black diamonds mark the number of unique studies with
decreased-profile support. This separates raw profile recurrence from recurrence
across independent studies.

All Parkinson's follow-up diagrams are rendered with Matplotlib and exported as
both PNG and SVG. Orange encodes increased-profile support, blue encodes
decreased-profile support, and black diamonds mark means or decreased-study
support depending on the panel. Companion TSV files store the profile-level
scores, direction summaries, paired contrasts, statistical summaries, target-taxa
direction rows, and target-taxa rollups.

### Supplemental Paired Profile-Contrast Infographic

`trait_profile_paired_contrast_infographic.png` is an analogy to the
enrichment-analysis infographic but uses the full profile table from
`data/derived/study_trait_profiles_wide.tsv` and matched-pair metadata from
`trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_paired_enrichment_metadata.tsv`. Rows
are matched BugSigDB study/experiment pairs with the increased profile z-score
minus the decreased profile z-score for each eligible selected trait.

Panel A ranks eligible traits by one-sample paired t-test signal against zero
over the matched increased-minus-decreased rows. The plotted value is `-log10(p)`,
and bar color encodes whether the trait is enriched in increased profiles or
decreased profiles. The
companion table
`trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats.tsv` records the mean, median,
standard deviation, direction, p-value, and Benjamini-Hochberg q-value for each
trait.

The same script also writes explicit paired-contrast trait-stat tables for
downstream evidence-matrix assembly. `trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats.tsv`
is the backward-compatible pooled table, and
`trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats_pooled.tsv` is the same pooled result
under an explicit filename. Per-stratum tables are written as
`trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats_<body_site_group>.tsv` for each
body-site group with at least 50 matched pairs, with the primary stratified
analysis in `trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats_feces.tsv` and the
robustness run in
`trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats_feces_excluded.tsv`. The manifest
`trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats_manifest.tsv` records the emitted
strata, pair counts, study counts, and output paths.

The pooled table and each stratum rerun selected-trait screening rather than
reusing the 60-trait heatmap list. The selection first keeps traits with
sufficient annotation support among profiles represented in the analysis set and
then ranks traits by the existing variance-weighted profile-composition
selection score. No top-60 cap is applied to these TSVs; every eligible trait is
retained. The resulting tables record
`selected_trait_rank`, selection support columns, `n_profiles_where_annotated`,
and `annotated_profile_fraction` so downstream checks can separate lack of
signal from limited annotation coverage. The `trait` column is a stable trait
identifier suitable for joins, while `trait_label` keeps the readable display
label.

Panel B groups matched pairs by BugSigDB condition label after removing broad
metadata-like labels such as exposure, measurement, population, clinical
treatment, health trait, microbiome, and physical activity. It shows the average
positive and negative directional contrast load per condition, requiring at
least 10 matched pairs. The companion table is
`trait_profile_paired_contrast_condition_summary.tsv`.

Panel C groups matched pairs by explicit body-site group, excluding `Other` and
`unknown`, and displays the strongest anatomical contrast-load groups on the
same simplified body-outline convention used by the v2 guild body-site panels.
Bubble size is the number of matched pairs, color is the net
increased-minus-decreased contrast, and labels report the average absolute
contrast load. The companion table is
`trait_profile_paired_contrast_body_site_summary.tsv`.

Panel D states the interpretation guardrail: Ilyas's enrichment figure ranks
traits that are commonly enriched across profiles, while this paired profile
figure ranks traits whose selected profile z-scores differ directionally within
matched increased/decreased study-experiment pairs. Differences between the two
figures are therefore expected and are part of the comparison story rather than
failed replication. The p-values and q-values are descriptive screens over the
selected profile-trait matrix, not new differential-abundance tests.

### Supplemental Profile-Condition Bars

The profile-condition bars are generated by
`scripts/create_profile_condition_enrichment_bars.py` from
`trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_clustered_heatmap_matrix.tsv` and
`trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_study_metadata.tsv`. These plots do
not rerun the hypergeometric ORA from the enrichment infographic. Instead, each
selected-trait cell is counted as positive when its selected heatmap z-score is
above zero, meaning the trait fraction is above that trait column's dataset
average in the selected profile matrix.

The default run writes to `profile-condition-bars/`. Conditions are filtered to
remove broad metadata-like labels, then retained when they have at least 10
profiles and at least two studies. The depleted-condition focus additionally
requires at least three decreased profiles and a positive decreased-minus-increased
mean positive-trait delta. The default command uses `--focus-direction dec`,
`--sort-by depletion-bias`, `--min-decreased-profiles 3`, and no body-site
filter. The bar-style figures display the top 15 focus-condition rows after
ranking, while the scatterplot and condition x trait heatmap include every
focus-eligible row after the same filters. Optional arguments allow the same
workflow to be rerun for increased profiles or for a specific
`body_site_group`/`body_site`.

The companion table
`profile-condition-bars/condition_profile_trait_summary.tsv` records the
condition-level counts, direction-specific profile counts, positive-profile
fractions, average positive-trait depths, and both
decreased-minus-increased and increased-minus-decreased summary deltas.
It also records coverage-weighted signal scores, defined as direction-delta
times the square root of positive-signal profile count times the square root of
study count, as supplemental prioritization values.
The additional scatter table
`profile-condition-bars/condition_depleted_signal_vs_coverage_scatter.tsv`
records the uncapped focus-condition rows used for the support scatterplot, and
`profile-condition-bars/condition_depleted_trait_signal_heatmap_matrix.tsv`
records the uncapped condition x selected-trait matrix used for the heatmap.

#### `condition_positive_profile_count_bar.png`

This coverage plot ranks conditions by the number of profiles with at least one
positive selected-trait z-score. Each horizontal bar is stacked by BugSigDB
profile direction: decreased profiles in red and increased profiles in blue.
The x-axis unit is a profile count, not a study count or an experiment count.

### Shared Data Collection and Label Definitions

The figures use precomputed BugSigDB study-profile trait tables. They do not
recompute differential abundance from raw sequencing data. BugSigDB direction
labels are curated signature directions, specifically the `Abundance in Group 1`
direction represented downstream as `increased` or `decreased`.

`trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_clustered_heatmap_matrix.tsv` contains
profile-level standardized trait z-scores. Rows are BugSigDB study profiles and
columns are traits mapped from KG-Microbe and related derived trait tables.
`trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_study_metadata.tsv` supplies the
profile metadata used for grouping: study identifier, condition label,
experiment/profile identifiers, body-site labels, body-site groups, and the
curated profile direction. The z-score columns are therefore profile summaries,
not measurements from one taxon alone.

`data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv` is used as the KG-Microbe grounding table
for taxon-to-trait annotations. `data/derived/taxa.tsv` supplies BugSigDB taxon
occurrences, which are deduplicated by taxon and study profile before target
taxon occurrence charts are counted.
`trait-visualization/taxa_guild_discovery_taxa_metadata.tsv` supplies the
overall increased/decreased BugSigDB profile counts for fixed target taxa.

Important units and labels:

- `profile_count` is the number of BugSigDB study-profile rows after filtering.
  It is not the number of experiments.
- `study_count` is the number of unique BugSigDB studies represented by those
  profile rows.
- `condition` is the BugSigDB condition label. Some labels are disease labels,
  while others are study-design or metadata labels such as exposure,
  measurement, timepoint, population, or treatment context. Condition labels can
  also be comma-joined labels.
- `body_site_group` is a coarse display grouping derived from BugSigDB body-site
  labels. `unknown` means no specific body-site label was available.
- `mean_guild_z` is the backward-compatible column name for the mean
  profile-level trait-enrichment z-score across all profiles in a group.
- `increased_mean_guild_z` and `decreased_mean_guild_z` are
  backward-compatible column names for increased-profile and decreased-profile group means.
- `increased_minus_decreased_guild_z` is the backward-compatible column name for
  the increased-minus-decreased contrast in the aerobe v2 parent story.
- `decreased_minus_increased_guild_z` is the backward-compatible column name for
  the decreased-minus-increased contrast in the anaerobe parent story.
- `paired d` is a paired standardized mean difference calculated from matched
  increased/decreased profile pairs within the same study and experiment.
  `paired d=NA` means fewer than three matched pairs or zero variance among the
  paired differences.
- `p` is a two-sided paired t-test p-value for the same matched differences.
  These p-values are descriptive labels on the screening plots, not a multiple
  testing-adjusted discovery procedure.
- `target taxon-profile occurrence` means one fixed focal taxon observed in one
  BugSigDB study profile after taxon/profile deduplication. If several focal
  taxa occur in the same profile, that profile can contribute several
  occurrences to a target-taxon condition chart.

The condition trait-enrichment bar charts remove a defined set of broad condition labels
before display. The exclusion rules remove exact labels such as `Exposure`,
`Population`, `Physical activity`, `Body fat distribution`, `Health trait`, and
`Microbiome`; the anaerobe script also removes `Clinical treatment`. Labels
containing `exposure`, `measurement`, or `population` are also excluded. Labels
such as `Timepoint`, `Diet`, or `Health study participation` can still appear if
they are not in the explicit exclusion list, so they should be interpreted as
metadata-like contexts unless manually reviewed.

### Trait Selection and Parent Hypotheses

The v2 trait sets use a priori KG-Microbe oxygen-tolerance traits rather than a
taxon list, top condition list, or post hoc selected enrichment result. The
selected traits are recorded in each folder's `guild_trait_selection.tsv`,
including trait IDs, focus direction, comparator direction, a short selection
justification, and the number of taxa in `data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv`
carrying each selected trait.

The parent hypotheses are kept explicit:

- Aerobe parent focus: aerobe traits enriched in increased profiles.
- Anaerobe parent focus: anaerobe traits enriched in decreased profiles.

The aerobe v2 trait-enrichment score is the row-wise mean of three
oxygen-tolerance trait z-scores:

| trait ID | trait label | KG-Microbe taxa with trait | selection role |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| `METPO:1000608` | facultatively aerobic | 1,092 | facultatively aerobic oxygen-tolerance concept |
| `METPO:1000602` | aerobic | 2,146 | oxygen-positive growth trait |
| `METPO:1000604` | microaerophilic | 1,016 | low-oxygen growth trait kept within oxygen tolerance |

The anaerobe v2 trait-enrichment score is the row-wise mean of two
oxygen-tolerance counterpart trait z-scores:

| trait ID | trait label | KG-Microbe taxa with trait | selection role |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| `METPO:1000603` | anaerobic | 1,942 | oxygen-tolerance counterpart selected for decreased profiles |
| `METPO:1000607` | obligately anaerobic | 352 | stricter anaerobe concept, avoiding fermentation metabolism |

Target-taxon panels are audit views, not the guild definition. They use fixed
NCBI Taxonomy IDs to ask whether named taxa recur in the expected BugSigDB
direction and condition contexts. Trait-enrichment panels instead summarize
selected trait-set scores across all taxa contributing to the profile-level
trait matrix.

### Interpretation Guardrails

These guardrails apply to all slides:

- Trait-enrichment figures summarize profile-level trait z-scores, not new
  differential-abundance estimates from raw sequencing data.
- Target-taxon direction and condition panels are audit views for fixed focal
  NCBI Taxonomy IDs. They should not be interpreted as recomputed trait
  enrichment.
- Condition-label rows are BugSigDB label rows after filtering. They are not
  experiments, and they can include metadata-like labels.
- Body-site panels are required context because oxygen-tolerance signals can be
  driven by oral, airway, lower-gut, urogenital, unknown, or mixed body-site
  profiles.
- The v2 parent comparison is intentionally simple: aerobe traits enriched in
  increased profiles and anaerobe traits enriched in decreased profiles. Off-diagonal cells are
  guardrails for direction specificity.
- KG-Microbe grounding means the selected trait IDs are connected back to taxa
  carrying those annotations in `data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv`; it does
  not mean every observed profile taxon is experimentally validated in the same
  study.

#### Shared Inputs

Study-profile z-scores were read from `trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_clustered_heatmap_matrix.tsv` and joined to study metadata from `trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_study_metadata.tsv`. The aerobe v2 trait-enrichment score was the row-wise mean of the selected traits: facultatively aerobic, aerobic, and microaerophilic.
`guild_trait_selection.tsv` documents the a priori selected trait set, the focus/comparator directions, a short selection justification for each trait, and KG-Microbe grounding as the count of taxa carrying each trait in `data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv`.
Direction labels (`increased` and `decreased`) were taken from BugSigDB's curated `Abundance in Group 1` signature field. This script did not recalculate differential abundance; it grouped and filtered BugSigDB-reported study/experiment/direction profiles by condition and body-site metadata.

The focal taxa were defined a priori in the script's `TARGET_TAXA` map using NCBI Taxonomy identifiers, which avoids ambiguity from synonym or strain-name differences. The selected taxa were Parvimonas micra (NCBITaxon:33033), Klebsiella pneumoniae (NCBITaxon:573), Streptococcus anginosus (NCBITaxon:1328), Escherichia coli (NCBITaxon:562), Fusobacterium nucleatum (NCBITaxon:851), Enterocloster bolteae (NCBITaxon:208479), Eggerthella lenta (NCBITaxon:84112), Prevotella intermedia (NCBITaxon:28131), Porphyromonas gingivalis (NCBITaxon:837), and Veillonella dispar (NCBITaxon:39778). They were chosen as focal examples for the aerobe v2 increased-profile trait-enrichment signal because they include taxa with oxygen-tolerance annotations in the derived taxa-trait matrix, and because they provide gut, oral, and airway contexts where aerobe or aerotolerant increased-profile patterns may be relevant. The script writes `candidate_taxa_trait_presence.tsv` from `data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv` as the audit trail for those trait annotations; some focal taxa carry broad multi-trait support, while others are retained as single-trait focal taxa for condition/body-site follow-up.

#### Shared Inputs

Study-profile z-scores were read from `trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_clustered_heatmap_matrix.tsv` and joined to study metadata from `trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_study_metadata.tsv`. The anaerobe v2 trait-enrichment score was the row-wise mean of the selected oxygen-tolerance counterpart traits: anaerobic and obligately anaerobic. This makes the comparison parallel to the aerobe v2 trait set, with the focus direction switched to decreased profiles. Profiles from all body sites are retained, and body-site summaries are used to separate gut, oral, airway, urogenital, and other contexts.
`guild_trait_selection.tsv` documents the a priori selected trait set, the focus/comparator directions, a short selection justification for each trait, and KG-Microbe grounding as the count of taxa carrying each trait in `data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv`.
Direction labels (`increased` and `decreased`) were taken from BugSigDB's curated `Abundance in Group 1` signature field. This script did not recalculate differential abundance; it grouped and filtered BugSigDB-reported study/experiment/direction profiles by condition and body-site metadata.
For profile-enrichment filtering, a profile's `enriched_guild_trait_count` is the number of selected anaerobe v2 traits with a positive z-score in that profile. The enrichment-filtered trait/condition histogram pair uses that same retained-profile universe. The trait histogram asks how many retained profiles have each selected heatmap trait above zero; the condition histogram asks how many retained profiles are assigned to each condition.

The focal taxa were defined a priori in the script's `TARGET_TAXA` map using NCBI Taxonomy identifiers, which avoids ambiguity from synonym or strain-name differences. The selected taxa were Faecalibacterium prausnitzii (NCBITaxon:853), Anaerobutyricum hallii (NCBITaxon:39488), Lachnospira eligens (NCBITaxon:39485), Agathobacter rectalis (NCBITaxon:39491), Roseburia intestinalis (NCBITaxon:166486), Ruminococcus bromii (NCBITaxon:40518), and Roseburia inulinivorans (NCBITaxon:360807). The script writes `candidate_taxa_trait_presence.tsv` from `data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv` as the audit trail for those trait annotations.
