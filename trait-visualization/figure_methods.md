# Figure Methods

This is the canonical consolidated methods file for figures in `trait-visualization/` and downstream outputs in `trait-visualization/trait-profile-investigation/`. It accumulates the methods text that previously lived in separate `*figure_methods.md` files, while preserving the organization by analysis family, current presentation figures, supplemental outputs, and archived source-method appendices.

Terminology convention: traits are described as enriched or depleted. BugSigDB profile directions are described as increased and decreased profiles. Backward-compatible filenames, folder names, TSV columns, and raw BugSigDB source labels may still contain `guild`, `depletion`, `inc`, or `dec` when those strings are part of an existing artifact or source identifier.

The figures were generated from BugSigDB-derived taxa and study-profile tables plus KG-Microbe trait annotations. Direction labels come from BugSigDB curation, specifically the `Abundance in Group 1` signature direction; the visualization scripts group, filter, aggregate, and cluster those curated directions rather than recalculating differential abundance.

## Clustered Heatmaps

### `taxa_guild_discovery_clustered_heatmap.png`

This figure is a taxa-by-trait clustered heatmap for discovering groups of
BugSigDB-relevant taxa with similar biological, ecological, or phenotypic trait
annotations. Rows are selected BugSigDB leaf-rank taxa and columns are KG-Microbe
trait columns. The current rendered matrix contains 80 species-level taxa and 45
traits.

The figure was generated by `scripts/create_taxa_guild_heatmap.py`. The script reads
BugSigDB taxon occurrences from `data/raw/full_dump.csv` when available, using the raw
export to parse taxon names, taxonomic ranks, study IDs, experiment IDs, signature IDs,
and BugSigDB abundance direction labels. If the raw export is unavailable, it falls
back to `data/derived/taxa.tsv`. Taxon-trait annotations are read from
`data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv`, and trait labels/categories are read from
`data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix_columns.tsv`. The script also reads
`data/derived/study_trait_profiles_wide.tsv` to require that selected traits have
support in study-profile data, not only in the taxa-trait matrix.

The script requests `strain` rows by default and falls back to `species` when strain
rows are absent; the current BugSigDB raw export contains no MetaPhlAn `t__` strain
leaves, so this heatmap uses species-level taxa. Full-dump rows are restricted to
`Homo sapiens` experiments before taxon support is summarized. Taxa were filtered to
those with at least 4 BugSigDB study/experiment/direction profiles and at least 5
annotated traits, then ranked by BugSigDB profile count, study count, and trait count.
At most 80 taxa are plotted. Traits were filtered to those present in at least 4
selected taxa and at least 4 study profiles. MediaDive growth-medium traits are excluded unless
`--include-media-traits` is supplied. Trait ranking uses
`selected-taxa support * log1p(study-profile support)`, and the selected matrix is
binary: `1` means the trait is annotated for the taxon and `0` means absent or not
annotated.

Rows and columns were clustered with seaborn `clustermap` using average linkage and
Jaccard distance on the binary matrix. Teal cells indicate trait presence and white
cells indicate absence/no annotation. Row colors summarize each taxon's overall
BugSigDB direction tendency across profiles: `increased` if increased profiles are at
least 2 and at least 1.25 times decreased profiles, `decreased` by the analogous rule,
and `mixed` otherwise. Column colors show trait category.

### `study_trait_profile_clustered_heatmap.png`

This figure clusters BugSigDB study/experiment/direction profiles by aggregate trait
composition. Each source row represents one study, one experiment, and one curated
profile direction (`increased` or `decreased`; stored in profile IDs as
`inc` or `dec`), so increased and decreased profiles are not merged before
clustering. The current full matrix contains 5,829 human-host profiles from 1,080
studies and 60 selected traits. The PNG rendering averages adjacent clustered profiles into 320
direction-specific display bins so that the full dataset remains legible.

The figure was generated by `scripts/create_study_trait_profile_heatmap.py`. The
script reads one wide profile table from
`data/derived/study_trait_profiles_wide.tsv`, readable trait metadata from
`data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix_columns.tsv`, study titles and compact study metadata
from `data/derived/studies.tsv`, and condition/body-site/location/host-species labels
from `data/raw/full_dump.csv`. Profile IDs are built as
`study_id|experiment_id|inc_dec`. The `inc_dec` labels map to BugSigDB-reported
`increased` and `decreased` directions. Each profile takes the condition,
body-site, location, and host-species labels of its own experiment; BugSigDB
records one value of each per experiment. The host-species label restricts the
plotted profile corpus to `Homo sapiens`. The study's majority labels are kept
only as `study_majority_condition`, `study_majority_body_site`, and
`study_majority_location`, and `study_condition_count` and
`study_body_site_count` count the study's distinct labels. Earlier versions gave
every experiment its study's majority condition and body site, which mislabeled
1,112 of the 5,829 profiles (#555).

By default, all profiles are kept (`--max-profiles 0`) with no minimum trait-count or
total-signal filter. Traits are selected to emphasize variable, interpretable columns:
they must be present in at least 6 profiles, present in no more than 95% of selected
profiles, and have nonzero composition variance. MediaDive growth-medium traits are
excluded unless `--include-media-traits` is supplied. Traits are ranked by
`composition variance * log1p(total signal)`, and at most 60 traits are plotted.

For each profile row, raw trait counts are converted to fractions of that profile's
total trait signal. Fractions are scaled per 1,000 profile counts and transformed with
`log1p`. Each selected trait column is then standardized to a z-score across plotted
profiles. The matrix TSV stores these full, unbinned z-scores. In the PNG, the color
scale is centered at zero and capped at +/-1: red indicates a higher-than-average
trait fraction for that row, blue indicates lower-than-average, and near-white values
are close to the column mean. The colors are relative trait-profile signals and do not
mean good/bad or increased/decreased abundance.

Rows are ordered by direction section. Increased profiles are clustered as one block,
decreased profiles are clustered as a second block, and the blocks are kept separate
with increased profiles shown first. Within each direction block, row ordering uses
average-linkage hierarchical clustering with Euclidean distance. The PNG display bins
are produced by splitting each direction-specific clustered order into adjacent bins
and averaging profile z-scores within each bin. Columns are clustered with average
linkage and correlation distance. Row annotations show direction, grouped body site,
and grouped condition. Column annotations show trait category.

### `study_trait_profile_paired_enrichment_heatmap.png`

This paired companion heatmap applies the matched enrichment statistic to the same
selected study-profile trait z-scores. The script keeps study/experiment pairs that
have both an increased and a decreased BugSigDB profile. For each matched
study/experiment pair and each selected trait, the plotted statistic is `increased
profile z-score - decreased profile z-score`. Positive values therefore indicate
traits enriched in the increased profile relative to the decreased profile from the
same study and experiment; negative values indicate the reverse.

On a per-row (per-pair) basis, this matched subtraction eliminates study- and
experiment-specific baseline differences (such as extraction protocols, sequencing
platforms, or shared host backgrounds common to both increased and decreased arms)
prior to visual clustering. Note that across the balanced corpus, profile
z-scores are standardized to mean zero ($\sum z_{\text{inc}} + \sum z_{\text{dec}} = 0$).
Consequently, the aggregate sum across all $N$ pairs satisfies
$\sum (z_{\text{inc}} - z_{\text{dec}}) = \sum z_{\text{inc}} - (-\sum z_{\text{inc}}) = 2 \sum z_{\text{inc}}$,
yielding the algebraic identity $\text{mean}(z_{\text{inc}} - z_{\text{dec}}) \equiv 2 \times \text{mean}(z_{\text{inc}})$.
Thus, while pairing removes baseline offsets individually within each experiment vector,
the aggregate mean contrast directly reflects twice the increased profiles' average
standardized trait abundance.

The current paired matrix contains 2,335 matched study/experiment pairs from 959
studies and the same 60 selected traits. The full paired matrix is written without
display binning. The PNG rendering orders matched-pair rows by average-linkage
hierarchical clustering with Euclidean distance on the paired enrichment vectors,
then averages adjacent clustered pairs into 320 display bins by default. Columns are
clustered with average linkage and correlation distance, so traits are grouped by
similarity of their enrichment/depletion pattern across matched pairs. The color scale
is centered at zero and capped at +/-1 for display.

Companion files:

- `study_trait_profile_paired_enrichment_matrix.tsv`
- `study_trait_profile_paired_enrichment_metadata.tsv`
- `study_trait_profile_paired_required_trait_check.tsv`

The paired heatmap generator also requires three requested profile traits to be
present in the selected trait set: `mesophilic` (`METPO:1000615`), `gram positive`
(`METPO:1000698`), and `non motile` (`METPO:1000703`). If any of these traits no
longer passes the heatmap selection filters, generation fails instead of silently
dropping the requested check. The companion required-trait TSV records each
trait's profile support, selection score, inclusion status, and matched-pair
increased-minus-decreased summary.

### `condition_trait_module_clustered_heatmap.png`

This figure clusters condition/body-site modules by aggregate microbial trait
composition (**direction-agnostic study trait load**). Because increased and
decreased profiles within each study and module are summed before module aggregation
by default (`--direction all`), the heatmap reflects which traits are frequently
reported in studies of a condition/body-site module overall, **not** directional
enrichment or depletion in cases versus controls (#134). Pass `--direction inc` or
`--direction dec` to `scripts/create_condition_trait_module_heatmap.py` for
direction-stratified module heatmaps. The current matrix contains 80
condition/body-site modules and 60 selected traits; the selected modules contain
2,598 study/experiment/direction profiles from 572 distinct studies of the
human-host corpus. Their per-module study counts sum to 636 because 52 studies
have experiments in more than one selected module.

The companion diagram `condition_trait_module_guild_definition_table.png` is a
presentation-friendly replacement for the dense heatmap when the goal is to explain
guild logic rather than inspect every module-by-trait value. It uses `X OR Y OR Z`
for taxon support and supporting-taxa evidence, while profile and condition-module
signals remain continuous summaries: `mean z(X, Y, Z)` over the selected trait
columns. `X AND Y AND Z` should only be used for a deliberately strict definition
where all listed traits are required; it is not the default for broad KG-Microbe
phenotype guilds.

The figure was generated by `scripts/create_condition_trait_module_heatmap.py`. The
script reads `data/derived/study_trait_profiles_wide.tsv`,
`data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix_columns.tsv`, `data/derived/studies.tsv`, and
`data/raw/full_dump.csv`. Each profile takes the condition and body-site labels of
its own experiment, and only `Homo sapiens` experiments are kept. Increased and
decreased profiles are first summed per study within each `condition` and
`body_site` combination (the default grouping; `--direction {inc,dec}` keeps one
direction), and these study rows are then summed into modules. A study whose
experiments carry different conditions or body sites therefore contributes one row
to each of those modules, and a module's study count is the number of distinct
studies with at least one profile in it. Earlier versions assigned every experiment
of a study to the study's majority condition and body site (#555). Unknown grouping
labels are excluded unless `--include-unknown-groups` is supplied.

Groups are eligible if they contain at least 2 studies, at least 4 source profiles,
at least 8 traits, and total trait signal of at least 40. Eligible groups are ranked
by study count, profile count, total trait signal, and trait count, and at most 80
groups are plotted. Traits are selected from group-level composition values. A trait
must be present in at least 4 modules, present in no more than 95% of modules, and
have nonzero composition variance. MediaDive growth-medium traits are excluded unless
`--include-media-traits` is supplied. Traits are ranked by
`composition variance * log1p(total signal)`, and at most 60 are plotted.

For each module, raw trait counts are converted to fractions of that module's total
trait signal. Fractions are scaled per 1,000 counts, transformed with `log1p`, and
standardized within each trait column. Values are clipped to +/-2.5 before plotting.
The heatmap uses a red/blue diverging color scale centered at zero: red means the
module has a higher-than-average value for that trait relative to other plotted
modules, blue means lower-than-average, and pale values are near the column mean.

Rows and columns are clustered with seaborn `clustermap` using average linkage and
correlation distance. Row labels are formatted as `condition | body site | study count`.
Row annotations show grouped body site and module-size category (`2`, `3-5`, `6-10`,
or `11+` studies). Column annotations show trait category.

### `bsdb-taxa-only/clustered_heatmap_genus_top100.png`

This figure is an earlier taxa-only presence/absence heatmap. It does not use
KG-Microbe trait annotations. Rows are BugSigDB study/experiment/direction profiles
and columns are prevalent BugSigDB genus labels. The current matrix contains 300
profiles and 100 genera; 170 rendered profiles are increased and 130 are decreased.

The figure was generated by `scripts/create_clustered_heatmap.py` from
`data/derived/taxa.tsv`. The script filters taxa to genus rank, maps BugSigDB
`direction` values to compact `inc`/`dec` labels, and builds profile IDs as
`study_id|experiment_id|inc_dec`. Duplicate profile/genus pairs are collapsed so that
one genus contributes once per profile. The top 100 genera are selected by the number

The matrix is binary: `1` means the genus appears in that profile and `0` means it
does not. If more than 300 profiles remain after genus filtering, the rendered heatmap
keeps the 300 profiles with the highest genus richness for readability. Rows and
columns are clustered with average linkage and Jaccard distance. Teal cells indicate
genus presence and white cells indicate absence. Row colors show BugSigDB direction:
red for increased and blue for decreased.

prevalent genera recur across studies assigned to common BugSigDB condition labels.
Rows are conditions and columns are genera. The current matrix contains 40 conditions
and 100 genera; displayed conditions have 13 to 81 studies.

The figure was generated by `scripts/create_condition_taxon_heatmap.py`. The script

a larger fraction of studies in that condition contain the genus. Row labels include

### Parkinson's Disease Follow-Up Analysis

The Parkinson's disease follow-up figures are in
`trait-profile-investigation/parkinsons-disease/` and were generated by
`scripts/analyze_parkinsons_disease_trait_profiles.py`. They apply the two
recommended Parkinson's-specific follow-up analyses: feces-only trait-score
contrasts and target-taxa direction consistency.

The analysis filters the study-profile metadata to exact condition
`Parkinson's disease` and body-site group `Feces`. The current filtered dataset
contains 247 BugSigDB study/experiment/direction profiles: 128 increased
profiles and 119 decreased profiles from 55 studies. BugSigDB profile directions
are the curated increased/decreased directions already present in
`study_trait_profile_study_metadata.tsv`; the follow-up analysis does not
recalculate differential abundance.

Aerobe and anaerobe profile scores are computed from the same v2 trait selections
used by the corresponding trait-profile investigation folders. The script reads
the selected trait columns from
`aerobe-enrichment-v2/guild_trait_selection.tsv` and
`anaerobe-depletion-v2/guild_trait_selection.tsv`, maps heatmap display labels to
trait object names with `study_trait_profile_trait_metadata.tsv`, and computes
each profile's row-wise mean z-score across the selected traits. Positive-trait
counts are the number of selected trait z-scores greater than zero in that
profile.

`parkinsons_feces_profile_trait_score_contrast.png` compares profile-level v2
scores between decreased and increased Parkinson's feces profiles. Each dot is
one BugSigDB study/experiment/direction profile, boxes show score distributions,
and the dashed horizontal line marks zero. The aerobe panel is interpreted as
increased profiles having higher aerobe enrichment scores when orange values
exceed blue values. The anaerobe panel is interpreted in the opposite direction:
decreased profiles having higher anaerobe depletion scores when blue values
exceed orange values.

`parkinsons_feces_paired_trait_contrast.png` keeps only matched study/experiment
pairs that have both increased and decreased Parkinson's feces profiles. The
aerobe contrast is `increased score - decreased score`; the anaerobe contrast is
`decreased score - increased score`; the combined reciprocal signal is the sum of
those two contrasts. Positive values indicate the expected reciprocal pattern:
aerobe signal higher in increased profiles and anaerobe signal higher in
decreased profiles. Dots are matched experiments.

Profile-level comparisons in `parkinsons_feces_trait_score_tests.tsv` use
two-sided Mann-Whitney U tests between the focus direction and comparator
direction. Paired contrasts use two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests against
zero. These tests summarize recurrence patterns in curated signatures; they are
not causal models and do not adjust for study design, medication, geography, or
sequencing platform.

`parkinsons_target_taxa_direction_bar.png` summarizes Parkinson's feces rows from
the aerobe and anaerobe v2 `candidate_taxa_condition_direction.tsv` files.
Orange bars to the right count increased profiles and blue bars to the left
count decreased profiles. The left panel shows aerobe-enrichment target taxa and
the right panel shows anaerobe-depletion target taxa. Long one-sided bars
indicate stronger direction consistency for a candidate taxon.

`parkinsons_anaerobe_depletion_taxa_support.png` is a focused view of the
anaerobe-depletion target taxa. Horizontal bars compare decreased and increased
profile support.

All Parkinson's follow-up diagrams are rendered with Matplotlib and exported as
both PNG and SVG. Orange encodes increased-profile support, blue encodes
decreased-profile support. Companion TSV files store the profile-level scores,
direction summaries, paired contrasts, statistical summaries, target-taxa
direction rows, and target-taxa rollups.

### Supplemental Paired Profile-Contrast Infographic

`trait_profile_paired_contrast_infographic.png` is an analogy to the
enrichment-analysis infographic but uses the full profile table from
`data/derived/study_trait_profiles_wide.tsv` and matched-pair metadata from
`trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_paired_enrichment_metadata.tsv`. Rows
are matched BugSigDB study/experiment pairs with the increased profile z-score
minus the decreased profile z-score for each eligible selected trait.

Panel A ranks eligible traits by one-sample paired t-test signal against zero.
Because multiple experiment pairs frequently originate from the same publication or study
(e.g., 2,335 pairs derived from 959 studies), pairs within a study share technical and
biological idiosyncrasies. To prevent repeated experiments from artificially inflating degrees
of freedom, contrasts are aggregated by study ID (`.groupby(study_ids).mean()`) prior to
hypothesis testing. The plotted value in Panel A is `-log10(study-level p)`, bar color encodes
direction (enriched in increased vs. decreased profiles), and bar annotations report the
study-level effect and 95% confidence interval as `mean {m:+.2f} [{ci_lower:+.2f}, {ci_upper:+.2f}]`
computed via the Student's t-distribution ($SE = s / \sqrt{N}$, $t_{\text{crit}} = t_{0.975, N-1}$).

The companion table `trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats.tsv` records both study-level
statistics (`study_count`, `study_mean_increased_minus_decreased`, `study_median_increased_minus_decreased`,
`study_std_increased_minus_decreased`, `study_ci95_lower`, `study_ci95_upper`, `study_t_statistic`,
`study_p_value`, `study_q_value`, `study_minus_log10_p`, `study_abs_t_statistic`) and pair-level
statistics (`pair_count`, `mean_increased_minus_decreased`, `median_increased_minus_decreased`,
`std_increased_minus_decreased`, `ci95_lower`, `ci95_upper`, `positive_pair_fraction`,
`negative_pair_fraction`, `nonzero_pair_fraction`, `t_statistic`, `p_value`, `q_value`,
`minus_log10_p`, `abs_t_statistic`), preserving full backwards compatibility for downstream consumers.

The same script also writes explicit paired-contrast trait-stat tables for
downstream evidence-matrix assembly. `trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats.tsv`
is the backward-compatible pooled table, and
`trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats_pooled.tsv` is the same pooled result
under an explicit filename. Per-stratum tables are written as
`trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats_<body_site_group>.tsv` for each
body-site group with at least 50 matched pairs, with the primary stratified
analysis in `trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats_feces.tsv` and the
robustness run in
`trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats_feces_excluded.tsv`. The manifest
`trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats_manifest.tsv` records the emitted
strata, pair counts, study counts, and output paths.

The pooled table and each stratum rerun selected-trait screening rather than
reusing the 60-trait heatmap list. The selection first keeps traits with
sufficient annotation support among profiles represented in the analysis set and
then ranks traits by the existing variance-weighted profile-composition
selection score. No top-60 cap is applied to these TSVs; every eligible trait is
retained. The resulting tables record
`selected_trait_rank`, selection support columns, `n_profiles_where_annotated`,
and `annotated_profile_fraction` so downstream checks can separate lack of
signal from limited annotation coverage. The `trait` column is a stable trait
identifier suitable for joins, while `trait_label` keeps the readable display
label.

Panel B groups matched pairs by BugSigDB condition label after removing broad
metadata-like labels such as exposure, measurement, population, clinical
treatment, health trait, microbiome, and physical activity. It shows the average
positive and negative directional contrast load per condition, requiring at
least 10 matched pairs. The companion table is
`trait_profile_paired_contrast_condition_summary.tsv`.

### Shared Data Collection and Label Definitions

The figures use precomputed BugSigDB study-profile trait tables. They do not
recompute differential abundance from raw sequencing data. BugSigDB direction
labels are curated signature directions, specifically the `Abundance in Group 1`
direction represented downstream as `increased` or `decreased`.

`trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_clustered_heatmap_matrix.tsv` contains
profile-level standardized trait z-scores. Rows are BugSigDB study profiles and
columns are traits mapped from KG-Microbe and related derived trait tables.
`trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_study_metadata.tsv` supplies the
profile metadata used for grouping: study identifier, the condition and body-site
labels of each profile's own experiment, experiment/profile identifiers,
body-site groups, and the curated profile direction. The z-score columns are
therefore profile summaries, not measurements from one taxon alone.

`data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv` is used as the KG-Microbe grounding table
for taxon-to-trait annotations. `data/derived/taxa.tsv` supplies BugSigDB taxon
occurrences, which are deduplicated by taxon and study profile before target
taxon occurrence charts are counted.
`trait-visualization/taxa_guild_discovery_taxa_metadata.tsv` supplies the
overall increased/decreased BugSigDB profile counts for fixed target taxa.

Important units and labels:

- `profile_count` is the number of BugSigDB study-profile rows after filtering.
  It is not the number of experiments.
- `study_count` is the number of unique BugSigDB studies represented by those
  profile rows.
- `condition` is the BugSigDB condition label. Some labels are disease labels,
  while others are study-design or metadata labels such as exposure,
  measurement, timepoint, population, or treatment context. Condition labels can
  also be comma-joined labels.
- `body_site_group` is a coarse display grouping derived from BugSigDB body-site
  labels. `unknown` means no specific body-site label was available.
- `mean_guild_z` is the backward-compatible column name for the mean
  profile-level trait-enrichment z-score across all profiles in a group.
- `increased_mean_guild_z` and `decreased_mean_guild_z` are
  backward-compatible column names for increased-profile and decreased-profile group means.
- `increased_minus_decreased_guild_z` is the backward-compatible column name for
  the increased-minus-decreased contrast in the aerobe v2 parent story.
- `decreased_minus_increased_guild_z` is the backward-compatible column name for
  the decreased-minus-increased contrast in the anaerobe parent story.
- `paired d` is a paired standardized mean difference calculated from matched
  increased/decreased profile pairs within the same study and experiment.
  `paired d=NA` means fewer than five matched pairs or zero variance among the
  paired differences.
- `p` is a two-sided paired t-test p-value for the same matched differences, and
  is `NA` whenever `paired d` is. These p-values are descriptive labels on the
  screening plots, not a multiple testing-adjusted discovery procedure.
- `target taxon-profile occurrence` means one fixed focal taxon observed in one
  BugSigDB study profile after taxon/profile deduplication. If several focal
  taxa occur in the same profile, that profile can contribute several
  occurrences to a target-taxon condition chart.

The condition trait-enrichment bar charts remove a defined set of broad condition labels
before display. Both scripts remove the exact labels `Exposure`, `Population`,
`Physical activity`, `Body fat distribution`, `Health trait`, `Microbiome`, and
`Timepoint`; the aerobe script also removes `Time`, and the anaerobe script also
removes `Clinical treatment` and `Health study participation`. Labels containing
`exposure`, `measurement`, or `population` are also excluded. Labels such as
`Diet` or `Treatment`, and `Time` in the anaerobe condition summary, can still
appear because they are not in the explicit exclusion lists, so they should be
interpreted as metadata-like contexts unless manually reviewed.

The six traits were selected from the pooled paired-profile contrast statistics
in `trait-profile-investigation/trait_profile_paired_contrast_trait_stats.tsv`
using `q_value <= 0.001`. The selected traits are facultatively aerobic
(`METPO:1000608`), cellulose catabolic process
(`METPO:2000103|GO:0030245`), coccus shaped (`METPO:1000668`), sphere shaped
(`METPO:1000683`), arsenate reduction

5,829 profiles: 3,050 increased profiles and 2,779 decreased profiles.

For each selected trait, raw KG-Microbe trait counts were read from
`data/derived/study_trait_profiles_wide.tsv` and aligned to
`trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_study_metadata.tsv`. Counts were
divided by each profile's total trait signal, scaled per 1,000 profile counts,
log1p-transformed, and standardized to z-scores across the human-host profile
universe. Each of the six traits was then treated as a one-trait guild:
profiles with a trait z-score above zero are the positive-z profiles for that
trait, and profiles with z-score less than or equal to zero provide the
background count for that trait.

Condition-to-body-site edge construction follows the same v3 Sankey utilities
used by the oxygen-tolerance guild maps. For each trait and each focus
direction, profiles were grouped by condition and body-site display labels,
split into focus and comparator directions, and summarized as edge records.
The focus direction is either increased or decreased; the comparator is the
opposite BugSigDB profile direction. For a given trait, condition, and body-site
group, `n=` is the number of focus-direction profiles with positive trait
z-score, `s=` is the number of unique studies among those positive-z focus
profiles, and `bg=` is the number of profiles in the same grouped record with
trait z-score less than or equal to zero. Candidate edges with no positive-z
focus support were dropped. Rank scores combine positive-z focus profile count,
unique focus-study count, and nonnegative focus-minus-comparator z-score
contrast. Where possible, displayed Sankey edges require at least 3 positive-z
focus profiles and at least 2 positive-z focus-profile studies; the display
selector preserves body-site diversity before filling remaining slots by rank.

Four trait-pattern heatmaps are written:

Rows are condition/body-site pairs and columns are the six shared traits. The

asking whether focus positive-z support exceeds comparator positive-z support.
This makes darker cells correspond to smaller p-values. A cell is shown for
exploratory review only when `p < 0.01`, with at least 3 positive-z focus
profiles and at least 2 positive-z focus-profile studies. Rows are ranked by
significant trait recurrence, BH-selected recurrence, p-value evidence, and

Two primary signed direction contrast heatmaps are written:
`six_shared_traits_grouped_condition_body_site_signed_direction_contrast_heatmap.png`
and
`six_shared_traits_individual_conditions_condition_body_site_signed_direction_contrast_heatmap.png`.
Rows are condition/body-site pairs and columns are the six shared traits. The
annotation reports the increased-profile positive-z fraction minus the
decreased-profile positive-z fraction. Color encodes this same effect on a fixed
-1 to +1 scale; red favors increased profiles and blue favors decreased profiles.
Supported effects with finite two-sided Fisher exact p-values are displayed when
the absolute increased-minus-decreased positive-z fraction is strictly greater
than 0.01, including nonsignificant results above that effect cutoff. Support
requires at least 3 positive-z profiles in either direction and at least 2 total
studies. A dark cell border marks BH-adjusted `q <= 0.05`; the manifest separately
reports how many cells passed the nominal Fisher `p < 0.01` threshold. Unbordered
cells are descriptive effects. The figure legend reports the displayed condition-label,
anatomy-group, row, cell, and BH-border counts. The
signed contrast table contains candidate edge records rather than a complete
Cartesian grid. Its `blank_reason` is
`low positive-z profile or study support` for a present candidate that fails
support, `absolute effect <=0.01` for a supported finite candidate removed by the
effect cutoff, and empty (`NA` in the TSV) for a displayed candidate. A
condition/body-site/trait combination for which neither direction emitted a
candidate edge is absent from the table rather than assigned a blank reason.
Absent records, present under-supported candidates, and cutoff-filtered effects
appear gray in the image. A direction may have zero positive-z profiles while
the cell remains colored when total profiles exist, the other direction meets
support, and the absolute contrast exceeds 0.01.

Conditions are not selected with a top-N disease filter. Signed contrasts read
the full increased and decreased Sankey edge tables before graph display
selection, after upstream removal of unknown raw condition labels. Every
condition/body-site row with at least one displayed cell is included.
The grouped view applies the v3 clinical condition rollup rules; the individual
view retains the original curated BugSigDB condition label. Both use the same
grouped v3 body-site labels. These condition and anatomy labels are fixed upstream
categories, not names inferred from dendrogram branches or descriptions of every
row on a branch. Grouped conditions are assigned by the ordered, case-insensitive
substring rules in `v3_fully_grouped_condition_display_label`; the first matching
rule wins and unmatched labels become `Other specific clinical labels`. The
per-trait `*_profile_sankey_condition_rollups.tsv` files audit the raw label,
display label, applied rule, profile count, and study count. Individual-condition
ranked rows are grouped by body site, then ordered
by nominal-p trait count, trait coverage, BH-selected count, evidence,
support, and effect size. Full overview PNGs retain all rows. Both views also
have readable `*_page_NN.png` files with at most 24 rows each. Grouped pages
follow the corrected clustered order; individual pages retain the ranked order.
Their paths and counts appear in `six_shared_trait_signed_direction_heatmap_pages.tsv`.

The primary grouped heatmap is a single clustered diagram with a smaller
`_heatmap_preview.png` linked to its full-resolution PNG in the README. Compact
clustered companions use `_signed_direction_contrast_clustered_heatmap.png`
suffixes. Clustered views use average-linkage hierarchical clustering on pairwise-complete Spearman
distance, `1 - rho`; columns remain in the fixed six-trait order. At least 3
shared supported traits are required to define a pairwise correlation. Missing
cells are not filled with zero. Before distances are constructed, rows are
sorted lexicographically by their exact condition/body-site pair labels. This
canonical input order makes SciPy's choices among exact distance ties
reproducible under input-row permutations, but it does not make the tied
topology statistically stable. Therefore, the dendrogram is not cut into
clusters and no cluster IDs or branch labels are reported. A branch label would
only be shown after a predeclared, multiplicity-adjusted enrichment analysis
supported that label; the present dendrogram is an ordering device only.

The earlier branch-label, trait-pattern, cluster-row, and cluster-summary tables
are preserved in the [September 23 branch-label archive](trait-profile-investigation/six-shared-traits/archive/2026-09-23-branch-labels/README.md).
They document the prior clustering procedure and are not evidence for branch
labels on the current figures.

Rows with no defined Spearman distance to any other row are excluded from the
linkage and displayed in a separate `Insufficient overlap (not clustered)`
block. For the remaining linkage rows, a row pair with too little overlap or
constant ranks receives neutral clustering distance 1. The figure subtitle and
manifest report undefined-pair counts and fractions before exclusion and within
the linkage subset, as well as the exact-distance tie excess fraction, defined
as `(linkage pairs - distinct distances) / linkage pairs`. Inclusion status,
exclusion reason, supported-trait count, defined-neighbor count, canonical input
order, and displayed row order are recorded in
`six_shared_trait_signed_direction_cluster_tree_leaves.tsv`. The complete
linkage merges among included rows are exported in
`six_shared_trait_signed_direction_cluster_tree_nodes.tsv`.

The raw individual-condition tree is compared with the grouped-condition tree
in `six_shared_trait_signed_direction_cluster_comparison.tsv`. Each individual
condition/body-site vector is mapped to its grouped aggregate using the grouped
edge table's `raw_conditions` field. The table reports their Spearman
correlation and distance, cluster orders, the nearest individual-condition
neighbor, and separate counts of source and displayed individual conditions.
`grouped_contributing_individual_condition_count` counts distinct raw conditions
in the full condition rollup map for that grouped condition/body-site pair;
`grouped_displayed_individual_condition_count` counts only the individual rows
that meet heatmap display requirements and enter this comparison. A grouped
aggregate can therefore include conditions absent from the displayed individual
heatmap.

Ranks and diagnostic flags require a finite comparison with at least 5 shared
supported traits; lower-overlap comparisons remain exported but are marked
ineligible. The within-group 1.5-IQR outlier threshold is computed only from
eligible distances and requires at least 4 such comparisons. Lower-overlap
distances do not affect that threshold. A separate flag records nonpositive
individual-versus-grouped correlation among eligible rows.

The grouped aggregate includes the individual row being compared, so this is not
an independent comparison. One displayed contributor does not imply that the
aggregate contains only that condition; source contributor counts establish the
extent of the rollup. These are exploratory pattern diagnostics, not formal
biological outlier tests. The six-shared-traits README embeds the grouped
clustered overview and links directly to the full-resolution individual PNG.

Issue #256 / PR #257 introduced pooled, denominator-harmonized, study-weighted
fraction contrasts and paired log-ratios. It did not emit condition-by-anatomy
replacements for this figure. Those pooled quantities are not substituted into
the profile-level positive-z prevalence contrast here because the estimand and
sampling unit differ; the manifest records that scope distinction explicitly.

BH q-values are computed across the full cell table within each condition-label
mode. The companion table records raw delta, raw Fisher p-value, BH q-value,
signed `-log10(p)`, display and nominal/BH flags, ranked page number, and
clustered row order for each cell. Dark borders use `selected_after_bh`; the
explicit nominal flag is `nominal_p_value_selected`, while
`significant_in_heatmap` remains only as a deprecated compatibility alias.
Floating-point fields are exported at 12 significant digits to prevent
last-bit textual churn without implying additional precision.
The Fisher contingency tables count profiles rather than independent studies;
BH-bordered cells are profile-level diagnostics and do not establish
study-level replication.

#### Correction Family and Significance Terminology

Oral filtering retains the source 3,144-cell individual-view BH family. It does
not recompute FDR or relax significance criteria on the oral subset:

- **BH-adjusted q ≤ 0.05**: Significance borders strictly mark BH-adjusted
  `q <= 0.05` (`selected_after_bh`). Across the 83 displayed oral cells, exactly **0**
  meet this threshold. The single BH-significant cell in the individual-condition
  dataset is fecal.
- **Nominal Fisher p < 0.01**: Uncorrected significance is explicitly labeled
  `nominal Fisher p < 0.01` (`nominal_p_value_selected`), distinguishing exploratory
  raw-p screening from multiplicity-corrected discoveries. Exactly **2** oral cells
  pass nominal screening: `Colorectal cancer | Oral cavity / dental` × `coccus shaped`
  (effect +0.714, p = 0.003394, q = 0.587927) and
  `Parkinson's disease | Oral cavity / dental` × `moderately halophilic` (effect +1.0,
  p = 0.007937, q = 0.881907). Their nominal flags are true, but they correctly lack
  dark BH borders because q > 0.05.
- **Distinction from Approach 2 display filtering**: In Approach 2 trait-pattern
  heatmaps, nominal Fisher `p < 0.01`, `n >= 3`, and `s >= 2` act as exploratory
  display filters determining which cells are colored. In contrast, in Approach 4
  signed-contrast figures, all supported cells above the absolute effect cutoff are
  colored and dark borders represent BH-adjusted `q <= 0.05`.
- **Distinction from pooled trait selection**: The six traits were selected by
  pooled across-disease association, which does not imply individual-condition or
  oral subset significance.
- **Compatibility aliases**: Downstream exports and manifests provide
  `nominal_p_value_selected` and `nominal_p_value_cell_count` as primary fields, while
  retaining `significant_in_heatmap` and `significant_cell_count` as deprecated
  backward-compatibility aliases.

### Interpretation Guardrails

These guardrails apply to all slides:

- Trait-enrichment figures summarize profile-level trait z-scores, not new
  differential-abundance estimates from raw sequencing data.
- Target-taxon direction and condition panels are audit views for fixed focal
  NCBI Taxonomy IDs. They should not be interpreted as recomputed trait
  enrichment.
- Condition-label rows are BugSigDB label rows after filtering. They are not
  experiments, and they can include metadata-like labels.
- Body-site panels are required context because oxygen-tolerance signals can be
  driven by oral, airway, lower-gut, urogenital, unknown, or mixed body-site
  profiles.
- The v2 parent comparison is intentionally simple: aerobe traits enriched in
  increased profiles and anaerobe traits enriched in decreased profiles. Off-diagonal cells are
  guardrails for direction specificity.
- KG-Microbe grounding means the selected trait IDs are connected back to taxa
  carrying those annotations in `data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv`; it does
  not mean every observed profile taxon is experimentally validated in the same
  study.

### Source: `trait-visualization/trait-profile-investigation/facultative-aerobe-v2/figure_methods.md`

These method notes describe how each PNG in this investigation folder was generated by `scripts/investigate_facultative_aerobe_expansion.py`.

#### Shared Inputs

Study-profile z-scores were read from `trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_clustered_heatmap_matrix.tsv` and joined to study metadata from `trait-visualization/study_trait_profile_study_metadata.tsv`. The aerobe v2 trait-enrichment score was the row-wise mean of the selected traits: facultatively aerobic, aerobic, and microaerophilic.
`guild_trait_selection.tsv` documents the a priori selected trait set, the focus/comparator directions, a short selection justification for each trait, and KG-Microbe grounding as the count of taxa carrying each trait in `data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv`.
Direction labels (`increased` and `decreased`) were taken from BugSigDB's curated `Abundance in Group 1` signature field. This script did not recalculate differential abundance; it grouped and filtered BugSigDB-reported study/experiment/direction profiles by condition and body-site metadata.

The focal taxa were defined a priori in the script's `TARGET_TAXA` map using NCBI Taxonomy identifiers, which avoids ambiguity from synonym or strain-name differences. The selected taxa were Parvimonas micra (NCBITaxon:33033), Klebsiella pneumoniae (NCBITaxon:573), Streptococcus anginosus (NCBITaxon:1328), Escherichia coli (NCBITaxon:562), Fusobacterium nucleatum (NCBITaxon:851), Enterocloster bolteae (NCBITaxon:208479), Eggerthella lenta (NCBITaxon:84112), Prevotella intermedia (NCBITaxon:28131), Porphyromonas gingivalis (NCBITaxon:837), and Veillonella dispar (NCBITaxon:39778). They were chosen as focal examples for the aerobe v2 increased-profile trait-enrichment signal because they include taxa with oxygen-tolerance annotations in the derived taxa-trait matrix, and because they provide gut, oral, and airway contexts where aerobe or aerotolerant increased-profile patterns may be relevant. The script writes `candidate_taxa_trait_presence.tsv` from `data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv` as the audit trail for those trait annotations; some focal taxa carry broad multi-trait support, while others are retained as single-trait focal taxa for condition/body-site follow-up.

#### `candidate_taxa_increased_profile_tendency.png`

Guild-specificity: no. This is a fixed target-taxon direction-count view; it is not recomputed from the v2 trait-enrichment score.

This figure uses the fixed focal taxa listed above, then intersects their NCBI Taxonomy IDs with `trait-visualization/taxa_guild_discovery_taxa_metadata.tsv`. That metadata table supplies the total BugSigDB profile count and the number of profiles where each taxon was reported as increased or decreased; the figures report these source directions as increased and decreased profiles. For each target taxon, increased-profile tendency was calculated as `(increased profile count - decreased profile count) / total BugSigDB profile count`. The horizontal bars show this signed value for each target taxon. No condition or body-site filter is applied to this figure; it summarizes the overall BugSigDB direction tendency of the preselected focal taxa.

#### `condition_increased_guild_signal.png`

Guild-specificity: yes. This figure uses the aerobe v2 trait-enrichment score from the three selected traits.

Study profiles from all body sites were grouped by BugSigDB condition label. Groups with fewer than five profiles were excluded. Broad non-condition or metadata labels (`Body fat distribution`, `Exposure`, `Health trait`, `Microbiome`, `Population`, `Physical activity`, `Time`, `Timepoint`, and labels containing `exposure`, `measurement`, or `population`) were removed from the displayed condition summary. Those labels usually describe exposure, cohort, sampling time, or measurement metadata rather than a disease or condition context. The figure keeps the top 12 eligible conditions with at least three increased profiles, at least two studies, positive increased-minus-decreased trait-enrichment signal, and positive increased-profile mean trait z-score, using fallback filtering if needed. Conditions are selected and sorted by increased-minus-decreased trait-enrichment signal. Bar length is this enrichment-oriented contrast, and the right-side labels show the paired standardized mean difference (`paired d`) calculated from matched increased/decreased profiles within the same study and experiment, plus a two-sided paired t-test p-value for the same matched differences. `paired d=NA` means fewer than five matched profile pairs, or zero variance among the paired differences; in those cases the paired p-value is also `NA`.

Guild-specificity: yes. This figure shows the three selected v2 traits and their increased-minus-decreased profile shifts.

For each selected trait, the script calculated the mean z-score in all increased study profiles and the mean z-score in all decreased study profiles. Bars show `increased mean z - decreased mean z`; positive values indicate traits enriched in increased profiles.

#### `anatomical_sites_diagram.png`

Guild-specificity: yes. Color encodes the aerobe v2 trait-enrichment score among increased profiles at each body-site group.

Study profiles were grouped by `body_site_group`, requiring at least 10 profiles per group. The display excludes the generic `Other` body-site group. Bubble and bar length encode the number of study profiles in the body-site group. Color encodes the mean aerobe v2 trait z-score among increased profiles at that body site. Body-site labels were placed on a simplified, neutral front-view body outline using the fixed anatomical coordinates in `src/mtp/body_site_diagram.py` for airway/nasopharynx, mouth and oral cavity (side by side, #612), subgingival/dental plaque (jaw), saliva (parotid gland, beside the ear), throat/pharynx (neck), lung/bronchus (chest), stomach (subject's left upper abdomen), small intestine (mid abdomen), colon (subject's left), caecum (subject's right lower abdomen), rectum (low midline), feces/stool, cervix/uterus (pelvis), vagina/urogenital, and skin (forearm) labels (#620); a body-site group with no coordinate rule stops the script with an error instead of being placed arbitrarily. Bubbles are sized in the outline's own units, so they keep the same proportion to the body in every figure that draws it; leader lines start at each bubble's edge and run above the other bubbles; and the colorbar sits right of the longest value label, so it cannot cover the values (#612).

#### `target_taxa_condition_breakdown_bar.png`

Guild-specificity: no. This is a fixed target-taxon occurrence-count view; it is not recomputed from the v2 trait-enrichment score.

#### Literature-review and direction-comparison methods

The PD direction reference is a targeted narrative review updated 2026-09-22,
not a systematic review or meta-analysis. PubMed, PubMed Central, and publisher
records were searched using Parkinson disease, gut microbiome, each taxon name
or synonym, and direction terms such as increased, decreased, enriched, and
depleted. Eligible evidence was limited to human PD-versus-control fecal or
intestinal microbiome studies and cross-cohort human meta-analyses that reported
an explicit taxon direction. Animal-only, intervention-only, non-gut, and
severity-only results were excluded from direction calls.

Exact species evidence was preferred. When it was unavailable, genus or family
evidence was retained as an explicitly labeled parent-level proxy. Conflicting
eligible evidence at the matched level is classified as `variable`, while
association-only evidence is not treated as directional. BugSigDB direction is
defined from curated PD fecal profile recurrence: `increased` when the increased
count exceeds the decreased count, `decreased` for the reverse, and `balanced`
for a tie. `agrees` and `differs` compare those categories with directional
literature calls; they are descriptive labels, not statistical agreement tests.
The reference TSV and Markdown report preserve the matched term, taxonomic
level, DOI citations, evidence statement, and cohort/medication/taxonomy caveats.
