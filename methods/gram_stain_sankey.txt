Gram-stain directional Sankey diagrams

Gram stain classes were taken from KG-Microbe as METPO:1000698 (gram positive)
and METPO:1000699 (gram negative); both labels were read from the merged
KG-Microbe node table. The input corpus, filters, and per-profile trait
fractions are identical to those used for the oxygen guild heatmap: BugSigDB
2026-04-18_11:12_UTC, human samples only, matched increased/decreased experiment
pairs only, and MediaDive growth-medium trait columns excluded, leaving 317
traits over 3,736 experiments and 7,472 profiles.

Gram positive and gram negative annotations are close to exhaustive and close to
equal across KG-Microbe taxa, so counting how often each class appears on the
increased and decreased sides produces two near-mirror-image totals by
construction rather than by biology. Per-direction hit counts are therefore not
reported. Instead each study contributes a paired contrast: for one condition and
body site, the mean increased fraction minus the mean decreased fraction of that
gram class, averaged over the study's experiments. A study contributes to the
increased flow when that contrast is positive and to the decreased flow when it
is negative; studies whose contrast is exactly zero contribute to neither.

Flows run from condition to body site. Link width is the number of studies whose
paired contrast runs in that direction, and flows supported by fewer than three
studies are dropped rather than drawn. Four diagrams are produced, one for each
gram class and contrast direction, together with a tab-separated edge table
listing every rendered flow with its study count and mean absolute contrast, so
the figures can be audited without measuring pixels. With the settings above, 255
edges met the threshold.

The diagrams were rendered with plotly and produced by src/bsdbng/gram_sankey.py,
driven deterministically by `just gram-sankey`. This analysis does not reuse the
existing enrichment ranking tables, whose hypergeometric urn draws taxon-trait
assertions rather than taxa; no p-value from that procedure enters these figures.
The BugSigDB export timestamp is read from the dump's leading comment line and is
recorded in the edge table and in every figure caption. The pipeline code and
this description were drafted with the assistance of a coding agent (Claude Code)
and reviewed by the author.
