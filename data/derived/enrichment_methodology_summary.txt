# Comprehensive Methodology: Microbial Trait Over-Representation Analysis (ORA)

Microbial trait enrichment analysis was conducted using a high-resolution Over-Representation Analysis (ORA) framework to systematically bridge taxonomic shifts in the human microbiome with granular physiological and metabolic phenotypes. This pipeline integrates the BugSigDB signature repository with the KG-Microbe knowledge graph, enabling a multi-dimensional functional characterization of microbial community changes across diverse clinical conditions, anatomical sites, and experimental designs.

## 1. Knowledge Graph Integration and Semantics
The foundational dataset for this analysis was constructed by integrating BugSigDB signature data with the KG-Microbe knowledge graph. KG-Microbe serves as a semantically linked repository for microbial traits, primarily incorporating the Microbial Energy Transduction and Physiology Ontology (METPO) and growth requirements from the MediaDive database. 

### 1.1 Taxonomic Propagation Model
To address the inherent sparsity and varying specificity of taxonomic reporting in clinical studies, we implemented a hierarchical trait propagation model based on the NCBI Taxonomy. This model utilizes the "subclass of" relationship to inherit phenotypic assertions downward through the taxonomic tree. For example, if a genus is characterized as "obligately anaerobic" within KG-Microbe, this trait is automatically propagated to all descendant species and strains reported in BugSigDB signatures. This propagation strategy ensures that functional signals are not lost due to taxonomic rank differences between the signature and the reference ontology, effectively maximizing the informational yield from each microbial observation. In cases where traits are known to be variable within a clade, the model prioritizes direct experimental assertions over inherited ones, maintaining a balance between data density and taxonomic accuracy.

## 2. Functional Scope of Phenotypic Traits
The enrichment framework evaluates 2,654 distinct functional features categorized into two primary domains, providing a comprehensive view of microbial capacity and preference:
- **METPO (Microbial Energy Transduction and Physiology Ontology):** Encompasses essential metabolic capabilities such as carbon source utilization (e.g., glucose, acetate, lactate), oxygen requirements (e.g., aerobic, anaerobic, facultative), energy transduction pathways (e.g., methanogenesis, sulfate reduction, fermentation), and physiological attributes like gram-stain reaction, cell shape, and motility.
- **MediaDive (Growth Environment Preferences):** Captures the specific nutrient requirements and chemical environments known to support the growth of specific taxa. This includes preferences for specific media (e.g., MediaDive medium 104, 693), highlighting ecological niche preferences and potential nutritional dependencies within the microbiome.

## 3. Taxon-Trait Matrix Engineering
A comprehensive taxon-by-trait count matrix was engineered to serve as the reference universe. Each row represents a unique microbial profile (defined as the set of taxa increased or decreased in a specific experiment), and each column represents a specific functional feature. The values represent the absolute count of taxa within a given profile that possess the corresponding trait. This high-dimensional representation allows for the simultaneous evaluation of metabolic capabilities, physiological traits, and ecological niche preferences across the entire BugSigDB corpus, providing a "functional fingerprint" for every reported microbial signature.

## 4. Statistical Framework: Global Over-Representation Analysis (ORA)
For each microbial profile, a one-tailed hypergeometric test was performed for every trait in the matrix to identify significant over-representation. 

### 4.1 Background Universe Definition
The "background universe" ($M$) was defined as the total sum of all taxon-trait assertions across the entire study dataset (the sum of all counts in the matrix). This "empirical background" approach is superior to a theoretical background (e.g., all known microbes) because it normalizes the expected frequencies against the specific taxonomic and diversity biases inherent in the BugSigDB repository. This ensures that the ORA identifies traits that are enriched relative to what is typically reported in human microbiome studies, rather than what is biologically possible in all of nature.
- **Universe Size ($M$):** Total aggregated trait assertions in the dataset.
- **Successes in Universe ($K$):** Total occurrences of a specific trait $T$ across all profiles.
- **Sample Size ($n$):** Total trait assertions within the specific profile being tested.
- **Sample Successes ($k$):** Count of taxa in the profile possessing trait $T$.

### 4.2 Computational Optimization and Numerical Stability
The probability of observing $k$ or more successes was calculated using the hypergeometric survival function:
$P(X \ge k) = \sum_{i=k}^{min(n, K)} \frac{\binom{K}{i} \binom{M-K}{n-i}}{\binom{M}{n}}$

Calculations were performed using the SciPy `stats.hypergeom` library. To prevent numerical instability during the computation of large binomial coefficients—which can lead to floating-point overflow or underflow—an optimized recurrence-based fallback mechanism was utilized. This mechanism employs log-gamma transformations and integer-based recurrence relations to maintain high precision even at extremely low p-values (e.g., $< 10^{-300}$).

## 5. Multiple Testing Correction and Significance Filtering
To maintain statistical rigor in the face of 2,654 simultaneous hypotheses per profile, p-values were adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) False Discovery Rate (FDR) procedure. 

- **Significance Threshold:** A stringent threshold of **FDR q < 0.001** was enforced to minimize false positives in a high-dimensional testing space.
- **Effect Size Filter (Fold Enrichment):** To ensure biological impact and prioritize the most substantial associations, a minimum **Fold Enrichment** threshold of **1.5x** was applied. Fold enrichment is defined as $k / (n \times (K/M))$, representing the magnitude of the trait's over-representation relative to the expected frequency under the null hypothesis.

## 6. Metadata Enrichment and Directionality Analysis
Each profile was enriched with study-level metadata extracted from BugSigDB's `experiments.csv`. This integration allowed for the aggregation of functional signals across anatomical niches (e.g., Feces, Oral Cavity, Vagina) and clinical conditions. Furthermore, signatures were analyzed separately by directionality (Increased vs. Decreased), enabling the identification of contrasting phenotypic shifts—such as the depletion of beneficial commensal traits (e.g., butyrate production, obligate anaerobiosis) and the enrichment of pathobiont-associated features (e.g., aerotolerance, specific nutrient utilization) in disease states. This separation is critical for distinguishing between the "loss of function" (Decreased) and "gain of potential" (Increased) phenotypes.

## 8. Ecological and Clinical Significance
By transforming taxonomic lists into functional profiles, this methodology enables the identification of consistent microbial "traits" that transcend specific species names. This is particularly valuable in human microbiome research, where different taxa may perform similar ecological roles across different individuals (functional redundancy). The ORA pipeline identifies these conserved functional signatures, providing a more robust and interpretable link between the microbiome and host health.

## 9. Data Flow and Pipeline Architecture
The analysis was performed using a multi-stage computational pipeline:
1.  **Taxon Extraction:** Processing BugSigDB signatures to extract unique NCBITaxon IDs.
2.  **Trait Mapping:** Querying KG-Microbe and MediaDive to build the reference taxon-trait matrix ([taxa_trait_matrix.tsv](bsdbng/data/derived/taxa_trait_matrix.tsv)).
3.  **Statistical Scoring:** Executing the ORA scripts ([trait_enrichment.py](bsdbng/scripts/trait_enrichment.py)) to compute p-values and FDR.
4.  **Metadata Integration:** Mapping enrichment hits to study metadata ([experiments.tsv](bsdbng/data/derived/experiments.tsv)).
5.  **Visualization:** Generating plots using Matplotlib and Seaborn ([create_trait_enrichment_heatmap.py](bsdbng/scripts/create_trait_enrichment_heatmap.py), [plot_enrichment_histograms.py](bsdbng/scripts/plot_enrichment_histograms.py)).

## 10. Reproducibility and Data Availability
All analyses were conducted using a reproducible Python-based pipeline (v3.12+). The complete set of enrichment statistics—including raw p-values, FDR q-values, observed counts, expected counts, and fold-enrichment metrics—is archived in the master results table ([trait_enrichment_results.tsv](bsdbng/data/derived/trait_enrichment_results.tsv)). Prioritized findings and scientific summary statistics are available in the top-tier enrichment summary report ([enrichment_summary_top10.txt](bsdbng/data/derived/enrichment_summary_top10.txt)).
